# The Tale of Rusty the Janitor: script

This file is the source of truth for the comic. Each panel's art lives in `panels/pNN.js`, and its lettering (captions and balloons) lives in that file too. The table below sets the **id, page, span, viewBox, mood and wear** for every panel. Artists must not change span or viewBox unless they also update `index.html`. See ARCHITECTURE.md.

## Cast

- **Russell "Rusty" Pruitt**, 61. Night custodian at Hollis Creek Elementary for thirty-one years. Soft-spoken. His red hair has faded to a rust-grey fringe, and he has a walrus mustache. Sober nine years.
- **Danny Pruitt**, 28. Rusty's son, with his father's nose and his mother's eyes. He left at eighteen and has not spoken to Rusty since.
- **Jess Pruitt**, Danny's wife, seven months pregnant.
- **Carol Pruitt** (seen only in a photo). Rusty's wife. She died when Danny was fifteen, and that is when Rusty started drinking.
- **The Unwilling** take the ones who will not come. They look like clowns: tall, too-long arms, cracked white greasepaint, pinprick eyes. They were refusers once themselves, painted and sent out to fetch the next one. See `tabs/clowns/` ("Don't call them clowns. Clowns choose it."). Rusty is not painted. He is one of the *kept* (`tabs/about/`), the ones seen years later "behind a counter, at the foot of a ride."
- **The rabbits** are things that used to be rabbits.

## Story in one breath

Rusty never hurt anyone. Ten years ago he showed up drunk to Danny's high-school graduation, and Danny told him never to call again. Rusty got sober. He never called. Then a letter arrives: *Saturday, noon, Mae's Diner. Jess is due in March. I think she should know her grandpa.* Rusty spends four nights carving a wooden rabbit for the baby.

Saturday dawn, the carnival's light finds him. Any other day, he'd go. He tears up the ticket and walks six miles to Mae's. At 11:58 he is at the window. He can see Danny, and in the glass he sees the reflection of the Unwilling standing behind him across the road, watching to see who he will lead them to. So Rusty lets go of the door handle. He leaves the rabbit on the sill with a note, *for the baby*, and walks away, and the Unwilling fall in step behind him. Danny waits until closing and believes his father failed him one more time. He finds the rabbit.

That night they come for Rusty. He is waiting in his good suit. Now he sweeps the carnival forever and mops away the blood. Every month, forever, he is Employee of the Month.

## Pages

### Cover
**p01 (splash, span 6).** A single carnival bulb hangs on a wire in black. Beneath it stands Rusty, seen from slightly behind, stooped, mop in hand, his long shadow falling on a floor slick with something dark. Far behind him the big top glows red. Title lettering reads THE TALE OF RUSTY THE JANITOR. Tagline caption: *He never hurt anyone.*

### Page 1: Hollis Creek
**p02 (wide).** A long school corridor after hours: lockers, kids' paper turkeys taped to the walls, one fluorescent tube flickering. Rusty mops toward us, and the floor shines behind him.
- CAP: "For thirty-one years, Russell Pruitt mopped the halls of Hollis Creek Elementary."
- CAP: "The kids called him Rusty. Most of the teachers never learned his last name."

**p03 (third).** Close-up of big, chapped, careful hands wringing a mop into a yellow bucket. A wedding band, worn thin.
- CAP: "He never amounted to much."

**p04 (third).** Rusty kneels by the LOST & FOUND box, pinning a single small red mitten to the corkboard so its owner will see it at eye level.
- CAP: "But he tried to do his part."

**p05 (third).** The janitor's closet: a hot plate, a dented thermos, and taped to the wall a faded photo of a freckled boy on a bicycle beside a woman laughing. A bronze 9-YEAR sobriety chip sits on the shelf beneath it.
- CAP: "Nine years sober."
- CAP: "Nine years too late for the only person it should have mattered to."

### Page 2: The Letter
**p06 (two-thirds, sepia flashback).** A football-field graduation. Danny at 18 in cap and gown, his diploma clenched, turns away from a younger Rusty, who has more red hair, is red-faced and swaying, and has a bottle-shaped bulge in his coat pocket. Folding chairs are knocked over and people stare.
- CAP: "Ten years ago. Danny's graduation. Rusty doesn't remember what he said."
- CAP: "Danny does."
- DANNY: "Don't come. Don't call. Don't EVER."

**p07 (third).** The letter, filling the panel, held in trembling fingers. Lined paper and a ballpoint scrawl:
> Dad, Saturday the 14th, noon. Mae's Diner on Route 9. Jess is due in March. I think she should know her grandpa. —Danny
- CAP: "Then, ten years later, the mail came."

**p08 (wide, night).** Rusty's one-room apartment, lit by one lamp. He sits at a kitchen table carving a small wooden rabbit with a pocketknife, with shavings everywhere. His one good brown suit hangs on the door, pressed, with a tag still on the tie.
- CAP: "He spent four nights on it. He'd never made anything for anyone."
- THOUGHT (Rusty): "Don't you mess this up, Russ. Not this one."

### Page 3: The Light
**p09 (wide, dawn).** The school parking lot at dawn after the Friday night shift. Rusty is by his rust-bucket pickup with his thermos. On the hill beyond the town, where there was only a cornfield yesterday, stands a carnival lit up in broad daylight: a big top, a Ferris wheel, and lights.
- CAP: "Saturday. Dawn."
- SFX (crickets, small): "chrrr chrrr chrrr"

**p10 (third, silent).** A cicada on a fencepost, rendered huge. In the background, the far carnival lights.
- SFX: "chrrr..." is struck through. There is no sound left.
- CAP: "And all the bugs went quiet."

**p11 (third).** A shaft of gold light cuts down through the dawn onto Rusty alone. A ticket flutters into his open palm: ADMIT ONE · TONIGHT.

**p12 (third).** Extreme close-up of Rusty's face lit gold from above, sweat on his scalp, eyes wide, then narrowing.
- THOUGHT: "Any other day."
- THOUGHT: "Any other day, I'd come quiet."

**p13 (wide).** Rusty tears the ticket in half. The light turns red. At the far end of the lot, under the dead light pole, one of the Unwilling stands too tall and waves slowly. It wasn't there a second ago.
- SFX: "RRRIP"
- RUSTY: "Not today. You hear me?"
- RUSTY: "NOT TODAY."
- UNWILLING (tiny, far): "...tonight, then."

### Page 4: Mae's Diner
**p14 (half).** Route 9, with Rusty walking in his good suit, the newspaper-wrapped rabbit held to his chest. Red balloons are tied to every mailbox along the road, and they all turn to follow him.
- CAP: "Six miles. The truck wouldn't start. Of course it wouldn't."

**p15 (half).** Mae's Diner, chrome and neon. The clock in the sign says 11:58. Through the window: Danny in a booth with Jess, his hand on her belly. Rusty's hand is pressed to the glass, and his face softens.
- CAP: "11:58."
- THOUGHT: "Look at you, son."

**p16 (wide, the turn).** The same window from inside. Rusty stands outside it, and in the glass's reflection, across the road behind him, three Unwilling figures stand in the noon sun. They cast no shadows. One points past Rusty at Danny.
- CAP: "And in the glass, he saw them."
- CAP: "Waiting. Watching to see who he'd lead them to."

**p17 (third, silent).** Close-up of Rusty's hand on the diner door handle, the fingers uncurling.

**p18 (third).** The wooden rabbit on the windowsill outside, with a scrap of paper under it: *for the baby*.
- CAP: "He'd done the one thing he came here to do."

**p19 (third).** Inside: Danny checks his watch, which reads 1:40. His coffee is cold. Jess's hand covers his.
- DANNY: "He's not coming."
- DANNY: "He was never coming, Jess."

**p20 (wide, dusk).** Mae's at closing. Danny stands in the doorway holding the little wooden rabbit and looking down the empty road. Far, far down Route 9, on the horizon line, a small stooped man walks into the red sunset, and three too-tall figures walk behind him at a respectful distance.
- CAP: "Danny found it when they closed up."
- CAP: "He never knew how close his father came."

### Page 5: The Taking
**p21 (splash, wide and tall).** Night. Rusty's apartment. He sits upright in a kitchen chair in his good suit, facing the door, hands on his knees. The door has burst inward, and carnival light floods in red and gold. Long gloved fingers curl around the doorframe, and one of the Unwilling's face leans in, grinning. Behind it, rabbit-things crowd the hallway.
- UNWILLING: "YOU REFUSED US, RUSSELL."
- RUSTY: "Yeah."
- RUSTY: "I did."

**p22 (half).** Rusty is dragged down the midway between two Unwilling figures. Around him a man screams with his mouth stretched open. Tents, and rabbits gone wrong.
- SFX: "AAAAAAAAAHHH"
- CAP: "Men screamed until their lungs gave out."
- UNWILLING: "The ones who come get to go HOME."

**p23 (half).** In the mud behind the big top, one of the Unwilling presents Rusty with a mop, a bucket, and a nametag that already says RUSTY.
- UNWILLING: "Most who make us come for them, we paint."
- UNWILLING: "You, we KEEP."
- UNWILLING: "Forever is a long shift, Rusty."

### Page 6: Forever
**p24 (third).** Under the screaming booth, Rusty mops a floor of blood. The bucket water is red. His suit tie is still on, filthy.
- CAP: "Every night."
- CAP: "The same night."

**p25 (third).** The inside of his broom-closet door is covered in tally marks, thousands of them, and they stop. On the shelf, a crooked rabbit whittled from a popsicle stick.
- CAP: "He stopped counting at 14,000."

**p26 (third).** A break. Rusty sits on an upturned bucket. Beside him a rabbit-thing hunches and gnaws something. Rusty talks to it softly. He is greyer and thinner.
- RUSTY: "My boy's got a little girl by now. Maybe two."
- RUSTY: "I bet she's got his eyes."

**p27 (final splash).** An under-lit stage after closing, with confetti, popcorn, and blood on the boards. A towering gold trophy, three times Rusty's height, gaudy and carnival-baroque, topped with a grinning clown figurine. Its plaque reads EMPLOYEE OF THE MONTH, with an engraved list beneath: RUSTY · JANUARY · RUSTY · FEBRUARY · RUSTY · MARCH ... running off the plaque. Beside it stands Rusty, grey and stooped, mop in hand, unhappy and resigned. He is not crying. He is past that. Off-panel, a gloved hand claps.
- SFX: "clap. clap. clap."
- CAP: "Every month."
- CAP: "Forever."
